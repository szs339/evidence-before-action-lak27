About the Workshop

AI-supported learning activities generate increasingly rich records of learner questions, revisions, help seeking, AI feedback, and interaction sequences. These traces can provide valuable evidence about learning processes, but they do not by themselves establish what a learner understands or what instructional action is justified.

Evidence Before Action is a hands-on design studio for examining the pathway from learner–AI interaction traces to defensible educational decisions. Participants will use a Trace-to-Action framework to connect learning objectives and interaction evidence to bounded claims, proportionate stakeholder responses, and safeguards.

Participants will analyze short, domain-accessible synthetic cases, develop a Trace-to-Action Canvas, conduct a structured peer Claim Audit, and revise their designs based on feedback.

Why This Workshop?

A learner requests several hints and then submits a correct revision. What can we conclude?
  • Did the learner improve their understanding?
  • Did the learner simply follow the AI’s reasoning?
  • Was the learner strategically checking an answer?
  • Did the AI provide too much assistance?
  • Is more evidence needed before taking action?

The same interaction pattern can be consistent with several plausible interpretations. The challenge for learning analytics is not only to capture and summarize learner–AI interactions, but also to determine what those traces justify us saying and doing.

Workshop Goals

1. Separate records from interpretations

Distinguish observed learner–AI actions from higher-level interpretations of learning.

2. Define the analytic target

Identify the traces needed to support a target and candidate indicator.

3. Bound the claim

Specify alternative explanations, uncertainty, and additional evidence needs.

4. Justify the response

Choose a stakeholder response proportionate to the available evidence.

5. Identify safeguards

Address privacy, fairness, transparency, and learner agency.

The Trace-to-Action Canvas

Participants work through seven connected fields. The Canvas makes assumptions explicit so they can be examined, challenged, and revised.

1

Learning Objective

What learning capability or process should the activity develop?

2

Analytic Target & Observable Evidence

What aspect of learning or activity will be examined, and what observable actions or artifacts could provide relevant evidence?

3

Trace Specification

What learner actions, AI responses, revisions, and contextual information are available or need to be captured?

4

Indicator

How will the specified traces be summarized or represented?

5

Claim Boundary & Uncertainty

What claim does the indicator support? What does it not establish? What alternative explanations remain?

6

Stakeholder Action

Who should respond, how, and under what evidence conditions?

7

Safeguard

How are privacy, fairness, transparency, and learner agency protected?

Participants also receive an Analytic Target Menu and an Action Menu to support Canvas development.

What Participants Will Do

1

Trace Interpretation Challenge

Examine synthetic learner–AI cases, distinguish recorded actions from interpretations, classify claims as supported, unsupported, or uncertain, and identify additional evidence needs.

2

Trace-to-Action Design Sprint

Complete a Canvas for a provided case or an AI-supported learning activity from the group’s own context, focusing on one analytic target and one candidate indicator.

3

Claim Audit

Exchange Canvases and audit whether traces support proposed claims, whether AI assistance or missing context changes interpretation, whether actions are justified, and whether safeguards are addressed.

Important: the Claim Audit exposes assumptions and evidence needs; it is not a substitute for empirical validation.

4

Revision & Cross-Case Synthesis

Revise the Canvas, document what changed and why, and compare revisions, contextual differences, safeguards, and unresolved questions across cases.

Workshop Cases

The workshop will use short synthetic learner–AI interaction cases informed by the organizers’ experience designing and studying adaptive AI-supported learning activities.

The cases will not reproduce or distribute raw student records. They are intended for workshop analysis and discussion; they are not validation datasets or benchmarks.

Each case will provide

  • a learning task and objective;
  • learner actions;
  • relevant AI responses;
  • revisions or other interaction events; and
  • contextual information needed for interpretation.

Cases will be designed to be accessible across disciplines. Participants will not need programming knowledge or expertise in a particular technical domain.

Participants will be invited to an optional follow-up communication channel and virtual meeting to discuss adaptations, implementation experiences, and unresolved questions.
